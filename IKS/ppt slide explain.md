# 🧑‍🏫 **Kaise Explain Karna Hai — Slide-by-Slide Script**

Neeche har slide ke liye tum kya bol sakte ho, simple language me:

---

## **1️⃣ Slide 1 — Title**

**“Language of Education in India – Based on Shreesh Chaudhary’s Article”**

👉 **Kya bolna hai:**

> “Good morning everyone. Today I will explain the evolution of the language of education in India, based on Shreesh Chaudhary’s analysis. India is multilingual, and our education history reflects major political and cultural shifts.”

---

## **2️⃣ Slide 2 — India’s Multilingual Landscape**

> “India has hundreds of languages and dialects. Because of this diversity, deciding *which language* should be used in education has always been a big challenge. This presentation shows how India’s educational language changed with time.”

---

## **3️⃣ Slide 3 — Sanskrit Era**

> “In ancient India, the language of education was mainly Sanskrit. Education happened in Gurukuls and Pathshalas. Only a small, privileged group had access, so learning was not universal.”

Point: elitist, limited, Brahmin-dominated.

---

## **4️⃣ Slide 4 — Persian Period (13th–19th century)**

> “During the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal period, Persian became the language of administration and education. Schools like madrasas used Persian. Again, access was limited to groups connected to the court or administration.”

---

## **5️⃣ Slide 5 — English Phase 1 (1603–1835)**

> “English first arrived through traders and missionaries. Initially it was not widespread. Only coastal areas and mission schools taught it.”

---

## **6️⃣ Slide 6 — English Phase 2 (1835–1947)**

(Macaulay Minute, Wood’s Dispatch)

> “From 1835, the British officially promoted English. Macaulay’s Minute introduced English as the medium for higher education. English created a new educated class, but it also created a social divide between English-educated elites and local-language speakers.”

---

## **7️⃣ Slide 7 — English Phase 3 (Post-Independence)**

> “After independence, English remained important because science, technology, law, and government relied on it. Today India uses a dual system: English + Indian languages.”

---

## **8️⃣ Slide 8 — Modern Indian Languages (MILs)**

> “Post-1947, Indian languages like Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, and others were promoted in education. However, English still remained dominant in higher education and jobs, creating an imbalance.”

---

## **9️⃣ Slide 9 — Problems in Education**

👉 Common problems Chaudhary highlights:

* Vocabulary mismatch
* Students think in mother-tongue but study in English
* Teachers are not equally trained in both languages
* Exams encourage memorization instead of understanding

👉 **Bolne ka tarika:**

> “Because the medium of instruction often doesn’t match the child’s mother tongue, students face comprehension issues, rote learning, and stress.”

---

## **🔟 Slide 10 — Social Impact**

> “Language creates social groups — the English-medium elite and vernacular-medium majority. Access to English often decides job opportunities and status. This creates inequality in society.”

---

## **1️⃣1️⃣ Slide 11 — Hope & New Directions**

> “Chaudhary says India needs a practical, inclusive approach. He recommends using the mother tongue in early education, teaching English as a skill, and reducing the English–vernacular divide.”

---

## **1️⃣2️⃣ Slide 12 — Conclusion**

> “In conclusion, the history of education language in India shows a constant shift — Sanskrit, Persian, English, and now Indian languages plus English. To make education accessible, balanced multilingualism is essential.”
